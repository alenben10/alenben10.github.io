I am a first-year PhD student in **Computational Biology at Cornell University**, supported by a SUNY Graduate Diversity Fellowship. I am interested in how machine learning can help us read biology more carefully — from protein language models that hint at enzyme function, to language-model pipelines that pull mechanistic evidence out of thousands of papers without losing track of where each claim came from.

Before the PhD, I spent a year as a laboratory technician in the Moghe Lab at Cornell building LLM-assisted literature-mining workflows, and a semester as a visiting researcher at UC Berkeley–USDA working on microbial genomics. My research started in Lima, at Universidad Peruana Cayetano Heredia, where I worked across immunoinformatics, protein modeling, and Nanopore metagenomics while finishing a B.Sc. in Environmental Engineering.

I care a lot about mentoring and about making computational tools approachable — especially for students from Latin America who, like me, arrive at computational biology from somewhere else.
